The week

Six working blocks. The calendar for your cohort is sent after the seat is paid. The order of the work does not change.

  1. 01

    Futures and leverage

    What a USDT-M perpetual is, how margin works, and why isolated margin is the setting we use in class. You will read liquidation price, mark price, and funding before you think about entries.

  2. 02

    Market structure

    Swing highs, swing lows, and the difference between a trend and a range. A level counts when price has already reacted there, not because a line looks neat.

  3. 03

    Price action on three markets

    The same read, applied to BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT, and SOLUSDT. We practice skipping more often than we practice clicking. Solana is in the week because it punishes a rushed entry faster than Bitcoin.

  4. 04

    Risk and size

    Position size comes from the cash you can lose, not from the leverage slider. You set a daily loss number and you stop for the day when it is hit. The Ksh 300 figure is worked as a goal against your own capital.

  5. 05

    Execution

    Order type, entry, invalidation, and one written note after the trade. If you cannot point at the price that proves the idea wrong, the order stays unsent.

  6. 06

    Your charts, marked by you

    You bring your own marked chart. We correct the process: the level, the risk, the reason you did or did not trade. Homework is a journal, not a pile of signals.

How a trade is built

You use your own Binance account. World Assets does not log in for you, does not hold your funds, and does not ask for withdrawal access.

Ksh 3,000 pays for the seat. Money you trade with is separate. It stays on Binance, in your name. Exchange fees and funding are also yours.

A classroom example, not a forecast: Ksh 15,000 on the exchange, Ksh 150 risked on the idea. A Ksh 300 day is two units of that risk, won, after fees. On a dead market the right result is no trade. Leverage can also take the Ksh 150 before the idea has had time to work, which is why you read the liquidation price first.

Sequence we keep

  1. Mark the structureTrend or range, and the level that matters.
  2. Name the invalidationThe price that means the idea is wrong.
  3. Size from the lossThe amount you can lose is chosen before the leverage.
  4. Read liquidationIf it sits near the entry, the size is wrong.
  5. Then enterOne order with a reason. Not a stack of maybes.
  6. Stop at the daily numberWhen the loss cap is hit, the session is over.
